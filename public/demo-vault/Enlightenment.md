# Enlightenment

The Enlightenment (roughly 1650-1800) was an intellectual movement emphasizing reason, science, and individual rights.

## Core Ideas

### Reason and Science
The Enlightenment elevated:
- Rational thinking over tradition
- [[Scientific Method]] over authority
- Empirical observation
- Mathematical reasoning (see [[Mathematics]])

### Individual Rights
Emphasis on:
- Human dignity and freedom
- Natural rights (life, liberty, property)
- Consent of the governed
- Equality before the law

These ideas shaped modern [[Political Theory]].

### Progress
Belief in:
- Human improvement through knowledge
- [[Science]] and [[Technology]] advancement
- Social reform
- [[Education]] for all

## Key Figures

Major Enlightenment thinkers:
- **Descartes**: "I think, therefore I am"
- **Locke**: Natural rights, social contract
- **Voltaire**: Religious tolerance, free speech
- **Rousseau**: General will, education
- **Kant**: Categorical imperative, autonomy

These philosophers built on [[Ancient Greece]] while creating new [[Philosophy]].

## Scientific Revolution

The Enlightenment embraced [[Science]]:
- Newton's physics and [[Mathematics]]
- Experimental method
- Rejection of superstition
- Quest for natural laws

See [[Scientific Method]] for the methodology.

## Political Impact

Enlightenment ideas influenced [[History]]:
- American Revolution (1776)
- French Revolution (1789)
- Constitutional government
- Separation of church and state
- Modern democracy

Study this in [[Political Theory]].

## Cultural Changes

Impact on [[Art and Culture]]:
- Neoclassical aesthetics
- Public intellectual culture
- Encyclopedias and knowledge sharing
- Salons and coffee houses

## Legacy

The Enlightenment shaped:
- Modern [[Science]] and [[Technology]]
- [[Ethics]] and human rights
- [[Education]] systems
- [[Political Theory]] and democracy
- [[Critical Thinking]] methods

## Critiques

Not without problems:
- Often excluded women and non-Europeans
- Sometimes overly optimistic about reason
- Tension between ideals and practice
- See [[Philosophy]] and [[Ethics]] for ongoing debates

The Enlightenment remains central to understanding modern thought in [[Philosophy]], [[Science]], [[Politics]], and [[Culture]].
